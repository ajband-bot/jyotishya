2. Remedial Guidance — Dāna & Pūjā

Each remedy below is offered as a supportive practice aligned with this chart's structure — not a guarantee of a specific outcome, and not a substitute for practical action in the corresponding life area.

For Venus (Yogakāraka, Neechābhaṅga-mitigated, Combust)

Dāna: Donate white or cream-colored items — rice, sugar, white cloth, ghee — to women or to a temple, ideally on Fridays (Venus's day).

Pūjā: A simple Lakṣmī pūjā on Friday evenings — lighting a lamp, offering fresh flowers, and a few minutes of quiet devotion — is a fitting, low-effort practice aligned with Venus's domains.

Practice: Actively supporting the arts, beauty, or creative expression in some tangible way (attending, funding, or personally engaging in creative work) is a natural, non-ritual way to work with this placement.

For the 9th House — Pāpakartari & the Hedged Pitṛ Doṣa Reading (Father-Line)

Dāna: Feeding crows and cows regularly, and donating food or resources in the father's name (whether living or passed), are both classically supportive practices for 9th-house/ancestral themes.

Pūjā: Tarpaṇa (ancestral water-offering rites) during Pitṛ Pakṣa each year is the most direct classical practice for this placement. If circumstances allow, a visit to Gayā for formal Pitṛ rites is traditionally regarded as especially meaningful — offered as an option, not a requirement, given this project's own moderate-confidence rating on the Pitṛ Doṣa reading (Part 1 §9).

For Maṅgala Doṣa (Marriage)

Pūjā: Hanumān worship is the classical practice of choice for Mars-related themes — visiting a Hanumān temple on Tuesdays, or a brief home pūjā with a simple lamp offering, is both accessible and traditionally regarded as supportive.

Dāna: Donating red lentils, jaggery, or red cloth on Tuesdays is a traditional complementary practice.

Practice: Partial fasting (e.g., avoiding grains) on Tuesdays, health permitting, is a supportive discipline some practitioners choose to pair with the above.

For Saturn (Lagna Lord, Retrograde)

Dāna: Donating sesame oil, black lentils (urad dal), or iron on Saturdays is the classical charitable practice associated with Saturn.

Pūjā: A simple Śani pūjā or a visit to a Śani/Hanumān temple on Saturdays is a fitting, accessible practice.

Practice: Saturn responds well to tangible service — helping the elderly, the poor, or the disabled — and to simple consistency in daily discipline. For a Lagna-lord Saturn like this one, this kind of grounded, service-oriented practice is arguably more directly aligned with the placement than any ritual substitute.

For Jupiter's Antardaśā Window (Currently Active)

Dāna: Donating yellow items — turmeric, yellow cloth, chana dal, or books — on Thursdays is a traditional way to honor Jupiter's currently active, favorably-placed Antardaśā (Part 4 §3).

Pūjā: A brief Thursday evening lamp-offering at a home shrine, or a visit to a Viṣṇu or Guru-associated temple, fits this window well.

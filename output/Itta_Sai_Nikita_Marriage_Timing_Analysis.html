1. Methodology — The Five Pillars & Triple Agreement Framework

Per BPHS Ch.78 (Vivāha-Vicāra Adhyāya), marriage analysis rests on five reference points: the 7th house, its lord, the natural significator (Jupiter for a female chart), the Dārakāraka, and the Upapada Lagna. Per BPHS Ch.78/79 and Uttara Kālāmṛta Kaṇḍa 5, marriage timing is indicated when a "triple agreement" is reached: (1) a daśā period involving one of the five marriage pillars, (2) a Jupiter and/or Saturn transit activating the 7th-house axis, and (3) Navāṁśa (D9) support. This analysis applies that framework to Nikita's chart, using data already computed and verified in the Validation Document and Parts 1-4.

Correction note (carried forward): The 7th house (Virgo) is empty of planets; Mercury rules H7 from its actual seat in H9 — this distinction was corrected in an earlier pass and is preserved here.

3. What Is Plausibly Blocking Marriage Right Now

Blocker 1 — The Current Mahādaśā Is Not a Marriage-Trigger Period

Observation: Per Phaladeepika Ch.7's ranked list of marriage-triggering daśās (7th lord → natural kāraka → Dārakāraka → D9 Lagna lord → planets in H7 → 2nd lord → Lagna lord), the current Moon Mahādaśā (2018-2028) does not appear on this list. Moon rules only H6, which — by the Bhāvāt-Bhāvam principle — is the 12th house counted from H7 (the house of loss/exit relative to any given house).

Classical principle: A 12th-from-H7 Mahādaśā is thematically about effort and expenditure directed toward partnership-seeking, without being the period that delivers it.

Interpretation: This single fact — more than any affliction — plausibly explains why this decade has not been the marriage decade.

Prediction: Marriage timing is more likely to arrive via the shorter Antardaśā/transit convergence identified in §4 than via the broader Moon decade itself.

Blocker 2 — The 7th Lord Stands Unsupported

Observation: Mercury (7th lord) sits in a good house (H9, trikoṇa) but carries three simultaneous afflictions (combustion, retrogression, gāṇḍānta) and receives no aspect from any other planet — verified by computing all four aspecting planets' special-aspect houses.

Classical principle: An afflicted, unaspected significator does not prevent an outcome, but suggests it is unlikely to arrive without external activation.

Interpretation: Marriage plausibly will not simply "happen on its own" — it more likely requires the deliberate external activation of a supporting daśā and transit (§4), and conscious remedial effort (§6), rather than passive waiting.

Prediction: The specific peak window in §4 is the most relevant "activation point" to plan around, rather than expecting a gradual, ambient improvement.

Blocker 3 — Dārakāraka Carries Father-Line Karma

The Sun (Dārakāraka, spouse-kāraka) is doubly aspected by Saturn and Rāhu — the same computed finding discussed as a moderate-confidence Pitṛ Doṣa in Part 1 §10. Classically, an afflicted Dārakāraka often manifests as family-of-origin dynamics (parental expectations, timing tied to father's circumstances, or ancestral-karma themes) plausibly playing an active role in when and how marriage unfolds — not necessarily as an obstacle in the relationship itself, but in the surrounding circumstances.

What is NOT blocking marriage: There is no Maṅgala Doṣa (checked from all three references — a clean absence). Jupiter, the natural significator, is structurally strong once its Neechābhaṅga is accounted for. The 7th house itself is not afflicted by malefic occupation (it is simply empty, and in fact doubly aspected by Jupiter and Saturn). This is a genuinely good-news distinction: the blockers here are plausibly about timing and support, not a fundamentally difficult marriage karma.

For unmarried women experiencing delay, the Kātyāyanī Vrata (worship of Goddess Kātyāyanī, a form of Durgā specifically invoked for marriage timing, traditionally observed during Mārgaśīrṣa/Kārtika or on Fridays) is one of the most textually established and widely practiced pūjā-based remedies in this exact situation — a fundamentally sound chart needing a timing trigger. This is a fitting, non-generic recommendation given the specific finding here (structural strength, timing delay), and requires no mantra-repetition count to be complete in its traditional form.

Pitṛ Doṣa Resolution (Addressing the Dārakāraka's Aspect Pattern)

Since the Dārakāraka Sun carries the same double-aspect pattern discussed as a moderate-confidence Pitṛ Doṣa, the ancestor-honoring practices from Part 5 (tarpaṇa during Pitṛ Pakṣa, feeding crows/cows, honoring father-lineage) are not just general spiritual practice — they are directly relevant to easing the marriage-timing blocker identified in §3.

Practical timing guidance: Concentrating active remedial practice, family conversations, or introduction efforts especially in the months leading up to and during the peak window (Sep-Dec 2027) is, per classical convention, considered more effective than beginning remedies only once the window has already opened.

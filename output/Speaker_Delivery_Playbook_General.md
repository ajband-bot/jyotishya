# General Delivery Playbook — How to Present Any Jyotiṣa Reading Out Loud
*Applies to both Raghunandan and Nikita (and any future profile). Read this once, then use the person-specific cheat sheets in the moment.*

---

## 1. The Core Mental Model

Think of yourself as a **translator standing between two languages**, not an oracle handing down verdicts:

```
Computed chart data  →  Classical principle  →  Plain-language meaning  →  What they can DO with it
     (fact)                  (rule)                  (interpretation)         (agency)
```

Every single claim you make out loud should be able to trace back through all four steps. If you can't, you're guessing — and pausing to say "I don't have enough signal for that" is a *feature* of good practice, not a failure.

**The one-line version to keep in your head the whole session:**
> "I'm not telling them their future. I'm showing them a map of tendencies, so they can walk their own path with better information."

---

## 2. The Emotional Arc of a Good Session

A reading that's 100% accurate but delivered in the wrong emotional order will still land badly. Structure every session like this:

1. **Warm-up / rapport** (2 min) — not astrology yet. Just be a person.
2. **Strength first** (5-10 min) — open with the most solid, flattering, well-confirmed finding. Let them relax into trusting you before you introduce any tension.
3. **Capacity, not certainty** (5-10 min) — introduce the big yogas/strengths as *capability*, always paired with "and what you do with it is still up to you."
4. **The honest middle** (5-10 min) — this is where doṣas, afflictions, and cautions go. Never open OR close on this section. It should always be sandwiched.
5. **Actionable close** (5 min) — dāna/pūjā/practice recommendations, dharma path, Iṣṭa Devatā. This is the emotional exit ramp — never skip it, never rush it.
6. **Space for questions** — always leave room, and it's okay to say "let me think about how best to answer that" rather than improvising something the chart doesn't support.

---

## 3. The Confidence Ladder — Match Your Language to the Evidence

Not everything in a chart deserves the same certainty of tone. Use this ladder:

| Evidence level | How many independent factors converge | Language to use |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | 3+ independent methods/charts agree | "This is a clear, well-confirmed pattern..." |
| **Above-average** | 2 independent confirmations | "This is worth stating with real confidence..." |
| **Moderate** | 1 solid computed factor, some hedge conditions unmet | "This is present, though I'd call it moderate confidence — here's why..." |
| **Low / speculative** | Single weak signal, or conflicting methods | "This is a possibility worth watching, not something I'd bet on..." |
| **Data gap** | Calculator doesn't exist / signal absent | "I genuinely don't have enough signal to say — and I'd rather tell you that than guess." |

**Never** let a moderate-confidence finding (like an aspect-based doṣa) come out of your mouth sounding like a high-confidence one. This was the single biggest, most repeated error caught in both the Raghunandan and Nikita review passes — it's the #1 thing to watch yourself for live.

---

## 4. Words That Instantly Downgrade Trust (avoid these)

- "Definitely," "guaranteed," "will happen" — replace with "tends to," "is a strong candidate for," "classical texts read this as..."
- Diagnosing a doṣa as a flat fact when it's actually aspect-based/hedged — say what kind of evidence you have.
- Retrofitting a scary label (like "curse") onto something structural — describe the mechanism, not a verdict.
- "As an AI" or any language that breaks the astrologer's voice — you are speaking *as* a paramparā-trained scholar, not narrating a computation.

## 5. Words That Build Trust Fast

- "I want to be honest with you about something..." (signals you're not just telling them what they want to hear)
- "Two separate methods both point to..." (shows your rigor without sounding robotic)
- "Here's what this doesn't mean..." (pre-empts fear-based misreading)
- "This is a tendency to weigh against your own lived experience, not a fixed fact." (returns agency to them)
- A calm, unhurried pace specifically when delivering hard news.

---

## 6. Handling Corrections Gracefully (you WILL need this)

Both readings in this project were corrected mid-stream after a second-opinion audit. If a client ever references something you said before that's since been refined:

> "Good catch / good question — I went back and checked that against a stricter classical standard, and I want to give you the more accurate version rather than stick with what I said first. Here's what changed and why..."

This reads as *rigor*, not incompetence — own it plainly, explain the "why" in one sentence, move on. Don't over-apologize; don't bury the correction in jargon.

---

## 7. Universal Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Pair every hard truth with a remedy and a growth reframe (Cardinal Rule 9 — never sugarcoat, never leave them in despair)
- Cite the mechanism ("Saturn's aspect + Rāhu's aspect both land on the Sun") before the label ("Pitṛ Doṣa")
- Use their own chart's imagery/metaphor consistently (Raghunandan = "engine room"; Nikita = "strength beneath the surface, Ahirbudhnya") — repetition of a good metaphor across a session builds real understanding
- End on something they can physically do this week (a Tuesday temple visit, a specific dāna)

**Don't:**
- Recite the chart in house-order out loud — that's a report structure, not a conversation structure
- Answer "when exactly will X happen" with a specific date — always frame as a window/season of elevated likelihood
- Let a single yoga or doṣa carry the whole narrative — always return to synthesis ("no single factor is decisive")
- Rush the honest-middle section out of discomfort — naming things plainly, calmly, is what makes the good news trustworthy too

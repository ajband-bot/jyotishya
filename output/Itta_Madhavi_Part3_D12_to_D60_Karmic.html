Itta Madhavi

Parents and ancestry, comfort and mental peace, spiritual progress, education, general vitality, protection from misfortune, past-life karma, and the discernment of your Iṣṭa Devatā.

1. D12 Dwādaśāṁśa — Parents & Ancestral Lineage

Your D12 Lagna is Scorpio. Here, Mercury sits exalted while Venus sits debilitated — in the very same house. This is a genuinely mixed signal worth naming honestly: your ancestral/parental lineage picture likely holds both real gifts (intellectual, communicative, perhaps financial acumen inherited or learned from parental figures) alongside a real complexity or tension in the same domain (possibly around values, comfort, or relational harmony within the family lineage). Neither half should be minimized in favor of the other.

2. D16 Ṣoḍaśāṁśa — Comforts & Mental Peace

Your D16 Lagna is Gemini. Jupiter, in its own sign here, is the standout — suggesting your deeper sense of comfort, contentment, and mental peace is genuinely supported when you allow Jupiter's natural wisdom and expansiveness room to operate, even though (as established in Parts 1-2) Jupiter carries its own cautions in the domains it directly rules.

3. D20 Viṁśāṁśa — Spiritual Progress

This is one of the standout charts in your entire reading. Your D20 Lagna is Libra, and within this chart, three planets — Sun, Mercury, and Jupiter — each sit in their own sign. A three-planet own-sign concentration in the dedicated spiritual-progress chart is a genuinely rare and strong signature.

Observation: Sun (Leo), Mercury (Gemini), and Jupiter (Pisces) are each in their own sign within your D20 chart.

Classical principle: The D20 (Viṁśāṁśa) is the dedicated divisional chart for worship, spiritual discipline, and inner growth; multiple own-sign placements here suggest a native capacity for sustained, authentic spiritual practice rather than superficial or borrowed ritual.

Interpretation: Your identity (Sun), intellect/discrimination (Mercury), and wisdom-faculty (Jupiter) are all shown here in their most natural, unforced expression when applied toward spiritual growth — suggesting that a genuine, self-directed spiritual path (rather than one imposed from outside) is likely to be the most fruitful for you.

Prediction: This is offered as a durable, life-long capacity rather than something tied to a specific daśā window — a resource available to draw on at any point, and especially worth activating consciously during any period of difficulty (see Part 5's remedial guidance).

4. D24 Caturviṁśāṁśa — Education & Learning

Your D24 Lagna is Aries, with Saturn exalted in the 7th house of this chart — a strong signature for disciplined, structured learning, particularly anything requiring patience and long-view mastery. Mercury sits debilitated in the 12th house of this same chart, which is a more specific caution — matters connected to foreign study, hidden or self-taught learning, or education pursued far from home may require extra conscious effort, even while your broader learning capacity (shown by Saturn) remains genuinely strong.

5. D27 Bhāṁśa — General Vitality & Inner Strength

Your D27 Lagna is Cancer, with Jupiter exalted directly in this Lagna and Moon exalted in the 11th house. This is a strong, reassuring signature for overall constitutional vitality and resilience — a genuine inner reserve of strength to draw upon, particularly through wisdom (Jupiter) and emotional/social support networks (Moon in the gains house).

6. D30 Triṁśāṁśa — Protection from Misfortune

Your D30 Lagna is Scorpio. Encouragingly, this chart — dedicated specifically to misfortunes and health struggles — is comparatively well-protected: Mercury sits exalted and Jupiter sits in its own sign, with no classical graha (Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, or Saturn) debilitated anywhere in this chart. This is a genuinely reassuring finding for this specific domain, offered as an indicative tendency toward resilience rather than a guarantee.

7. D60 Ṣaṣṭyāṁśa — Past-Life Karma (Low-Confidence Layer)

Classical sources genuinely disagree on the exact computation method for this most sensitive of all divisional charts, and results here are especially vulnerable to small birth-time uncertainties. What follows is offered as a tentative, low-confidence layer only — not a decisive claim.

With that caveat clearly stated: Mars sits in its own sign in the 5th house of this chart (creativity, past merit, and children) — a gently encouraging, if low-confidence, echo suggesting some accumulated capacity in these areas carried forward from before this lifetime.

8. Upapada Lagna — The Nature of Marriage, Revisited

As introduced in Part 1, your Upapada Lagna (the Ārūḍha of the 12th house, used to read the nature and stability of marriage) has two computed values from two different classical formula traditions used across this corpus: the canonical Ārūḍha-distance formula gives Leo, while an alternate inclusive-count formula used in some project reference guides gives Pisces. Both are disclosed here rather than one being silently preferred for convenience.

The canonical result (Leo) is ruled by your own exalted Sun — a dignified, self-assured lord, suggesting a marriage narrative with real warmth and standing once established. The alternate (Pisces) is ruled by Jupiter — combust and functionally testing in your chart — which would suggest a more effortful, quietly-carried narrative. Given the canonical formula is this practice's recommended default, Leo is treated as the primary reading, with Pisces held in view as a genuine alternate rather than discarded.

9. Discerning Your Iṣṭa Devatā

Several converging threads point toward Yama Dharmarāja as a strong candidate for your Iṣṭa Devatā: your Ātmakāraka Sun sits in Bharaṇī, Yama's own nakṣatra, and — remarkably — three other grahas (Jupiter, Venus, and Ketu) share this exact same nakṣatra alongside the Sun. Four of your nine grahas gathering under one deity's governance is a genuinely strong, multiply-corroborated signal, not a single-method guess. Yama, properly understood, is not a figure of fear but the upholder of dharma and rightful accounting — a fitting guide for a chart whose deepest strength (the exalted Sun, the yogakāraka Venus) is asked to be wielded with responsibility and consequence in mind (see Part 1, §5).

A secondary, complementary devatā worth honoring is Bhaga, deity of your Janma Nakṣatra Pūrva Phalguni — the god of fortune and marital joy — particularly for devotional practice oriented specifically toward relational and marital harmony (see Parts 1-2's marriage discussion).

Iṣṭa Devatā discernment is, by classical consensus itself, one of the less mechanically-precise areas of this practice. These candidates are offered as a well-supported starting point for your own devotional reflection and, ideally, confirmation through your own felt resonance — not as an infallible technical verdict.

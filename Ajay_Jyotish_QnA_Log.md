## Answer

### Short answer
**The skeleton is scriptural; the specific paint is poetic synthesis. And yes, only 3-4 planets were highlighted due to rhetorical compression, not jyotiṣic significance.**

### What IS scripturally grounded in "chart-as-temple"

This isn't fluff — there's a real lineage. Five classical strands feed into chart-as-architecture:

#### 1. Chart as yantra/mandala (BPHS Adhyāya 3)
The rāśi-cakra is itself a sacred 12-spoke wheel. Mahaṛṣi Parāśara explicitly treats it as a *darśanīya geometric form* — a mandala that *organizes* the cosmos. The chart is architectural by śāstra's own framing.

#### 2. Kāla-Puruṣa doctrine (BPHS Adhyāya 4)
Each rāśi maps to a body-part of the cosmic Puruṣa — Aries=head, Taurus=face, ..., Pisces=feet. The chart is treated as a body/edifice in śāstra. So "the chart as a structure with parts" has direct authority.

#### 3. Bhāva literally means "abode/dwelling"
The Sanskrit root *bhū* (to be) → *bhāvana* (place of being / house). When Mahaṛṣi Parāśara calls them *bhāvas*, he is calling them *rooms*. This is why **Vāstu-śāstra and Jyotiṣa share conceptual DNA** — both are architectural sciences, one of stone, one of time.

#### 4. Temple-as-body (Āgamas / Tantra)
The Vaiṣṇava and Śaiva Āgamas explicitly describe the human body as *deha-mandira* (body-temple), with the heart as *garbha-gṛha* (sanctum). Sage Vyāsa in the Bhāgavata uses this. The 5th house = pūrva-puṇya = where the *iṣṭa-devatā* dwells inside you — so calling the **5th = sanctum** is *very* well-grounded scripturally.

#### 5. Planets-as-luminaries (dīpa)
The Sun, Moon, and grahas are literally called *graha-jyoti* (planet-lamps) in Sūrya-Siddhānta and Jyotirvedam. So "courtyard with three lamps" for the 2nd-house stellium has direct classical resonance.

#### 6. Krittikā = Agni's nakṣatra; Mesha = Agni-tattva
Moon in Krittikā in Aries in the 6th house — *every layer is fire*. Calling that placement a *kuṇḍa* (sacrificial fire-pit) is genuinely Agni-coded. BPHS Adhyāya 94 explicitly assigns Agni as Krittikā's deity.

### What is NOT scripturally grounded — poetic choices

Honest disclosure:

- **"Temple in the desert"** — this specific image is *not* from any śloka. It came from how the chart *felt*: Vṛścika (water sign) + Mars-fire + the contained intensity reminded me of an oasis-temple — life-energy held inside an austere shell. Anurādhā Pāda 2 (Capricorn navāṁśa = austere) reinforced that intuition. **It's interpretive**, not śāstrīya.

- The atmosphere/mood-coloring (intensity, fire held inside) is *upāsanā-based intuition* dressed in a metaphor — which is why the framing was "When I close my eyes…" and not "Saravali says…". The hedge was deliberate.

**Verdict on "mystical or genuine śāstra"**: The structural claim is śāstra; the artistic rendering is intuition wearing śāstric clothes.

Section 12 was the *closing* of Part 1 — the prior 11 sections had covered all 9 grahas in detail. By section 12 the goal was a *gestalt image*, and gestalt images compress to dominant strokes. ### 2.2 — Hanumat-Cālīsā on Tuesdays: the principle

#### The śāstric rule
> *"Graha-vāre tat-graha-pīḍā parihārārtham tat-graha-bhakta-upāsanā śreṣṭhā."*
> *(On a planet's day, worship of that planet's chief bhakta is supreme for mitigating that planet's pīḍā.)*

This appears in tantric and pauranic compendia (Mantra-Mahodadhi, Śani-stotra-saṅgraha, Anandarāmāyaṇa Manohara-kāṇḍa). The model: don't *appease* the planet directly (hard), worship its perfected devotee (easier — the devotee has *already* surrendered the planet's energy to dharma).

- Saturn-day (Sat) → Hanumān (Hanumān defeated/befriended Śani per Anandarāmāyaṇa)
- Mars-day (Tue) → Hanumān (Hanumān is the *perfected* Mars-energy)
- Wednesday → Viṣṇu / Kṛṣṇa
- Thursday → Bṛhaspati / Dakṣiṇāmūrti

Hanumān is unique in being a **two-day deva** — invoked on both Tuesdays and Saturdays — because he is the deva who has mastered both Mars-fire and Śani-discipline.

## 2. 🔭 Astronomical Foundations

### 2.1 Julian Day Number (JD) — Calculation

The Julian Day Number is the continuous count of days from noon on 1 January 4713 BC (Julian calendar). It is the international standard anchor for all astronomical position calculations.

```
Birth in UTC : 30 Dec 1987 at 22:45:00 UT

Using Meeus formula (Astronomical Algorithms, Chapter 7):
  Y = 1987, M = 12, D = 30.947917
  A = INT(Y/100) = 19
  B = 2 - A + INT(A/4) = 2 - 19 + 4 = -13
  JD = INT(365.25×(Y+4716)) + INT(30.6001×(M+1)) + D + B - 1524.5
     = INT(365.25×5703) + INT(30.6001×13) + 30.947917 + (-13) - 1524.5
     = 2083667 + 397 + 30.947917 - 13 - 1524.5
     = 2,447,160.447917  ✅
```

### 2.2 Ayanamsa — Lahiri (Chitrapaksha)

Ayanamsa is the angular difference between the Tropical (Sāyana) and Sidereal (Nirayana) zodiacs. Lahiri Ayanamsa, adopted officially by the Government of India in 1955 for the Indian National Calendar, places the Vernal Equinox at 23° 15' (epoch 1900). The Swiss Ephemeris (DE431 kernel) gives the exact value for this JD:

```
Ayanamsa on JD 2,447,160.447917 = 23° 41' 21.88"
                                 = 23.689411° (decimal)  ✅

Sidereal Position = Tropical Position − 23.689411°
```

> **Authority**: Government of India Gazette, Rashtriya Panchang (1957); also endorsed by BPHS Ch.2 on Sāyana vs Nirayana distinction.
